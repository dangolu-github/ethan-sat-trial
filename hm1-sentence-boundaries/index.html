Clause-Skeletonone method for all questions
How to work
  1. Cover the choices first. Mark the subject and finite verb on each side of the blank.
  2. Classify the structure: Is each side an IC, DC, phrase, or list? Do not decide from a pause.
  3. State the rule: Name the punctuation allowed by that structure, then return to the choices.
  4. Write one rule sentence. For example: "IC + IC needs a period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS." Do not write "it sounds right."
  5. Finish and mark uncertain questions. Do not check answers. We will review them after submission.

Subtype 1: Independent-Clause Join

Legal joins between two complete clauses

Q1–Q9|9 questions
Method reminder

IC. IC | IC; IC | IC, FANBOYS IC

When both sides are complete clauses, use a period, a semicolon, or a comma + FANBOYS. A comma alone creates a comma splice; no boundary creates a run-on.

Subtype 2: Dependent-Clause Attachment

Attach a dependent clause to an independent clause

Q10–Q12|3 questions
Method reminder

DC, IC | IC + essential final DC (no comma)

A DC has a subject and finite verb but cannot stand alone because of a subordinator such as because, although, or while. Use a comma after an initial DC; use no comma before an essential final DC. A DC cannot stand before a period or semicolon as if it were complete.

Subtype 3: Extra Information

Extra information needs a matching pair

Q13–Q18|6 questions
Method reminder

…, extra, …　|　…— extra —…　|　…(extra)…

Use the removal test. If the sentence and reference remain complete, the information is extra and needs a matching pair of commas, dashes, or parentheses. One-sided punctuation fails. Essential information takes no pair.

Subtype 4: Colon or Dash Completion

Colon or dash: complete lead-in + explanation

Q19–Q21|3 questions
Method reminder

IC: explanation | IC — explanation

A colon or single dash requires a complete independent clause before it. Use the period test: the lead-in must be able to end with a period. The material after the boundary may explain, name, illustrate, or list.

Subtype 5: List Boundary

List boundaries: commas for simple items, semicolons for complex items

Q22–Q24|3 questions
Method reminder

a, b, and c | a, a2; b, b2; and c, c2

Bracket every complete item before choosing punctuation. Separate simple items with commas. If items already contain commas, separate the full items with semicolons. Do not split the lead-in from the list it introduces.

Subtype 6: No Punctuation

No punctuation: keep one grammatical unit intact

Q25–Q30|6 questions
Method reminder

subject + verb + object | essential modifier

A blank does not require a mark. Do not split subject from finite verb, verb from object, preposition from object, or an essential modifier from the noun it identifies. Two parallel elements do not automatically need a comma.
