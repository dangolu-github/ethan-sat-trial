Text Structure and Purpose

Today we connected sentence structure, precise word meaning, and the job each part of a passage performs. The next step is to apply those checks consistently before choosing an answer.

Class02
Class summaryFeedback and next stepsClass materialsHomework

What we covered

Nonfinite modifiers
  • Revisited the sentence skeleton and distinguished the main verb from modifying phrases.
  • Focused on passive nonfinite modifiers: identify what is described and whether it receives the action. The Shannon sentence helped separate “published” and “shortened” from the main verb “utilized”.
Vocabulary
  • Reviewed the Chapter 1 word set in Helpful, Harmful & In Between, including precise distinctions between beneficial, harmful, and neutral effects.
  • Practised explaining meanings in context rather than relying on a broad positive or negative impression.
What Is This Text Doing?
  • Main purpose: identify the topic, the author’s job, and the limits of the claim.
  • Overall structure: map the passage’s moves in order and check every clause of the chosen description.
  • Sentence or part function: read before, target, and after to identify how the selected part contributes.
Mistake review
  • Reviewed the earlier practice through Question 18, including modifier structure and reading choices.
  • Compared “superficial” with “ignored”, and heat resistance with heat exposure.
  • Used the smartphone-and-memory study to distinguish a reported finding from a question about whether the design supports the claim.
Agreement practice
  • Agreement in Complex Sentences was not taught in this lesson. Try its practice independently so we can see which structures need explanation.

Feedback and next steps

What worked in discussion

  • You stayed engaged and explained your reasoning during discussion.
  • You could identify useful sentence-skeleton information and recognize much of the vocabulary.
  • In the structure example, you questioned unsupported claims about credibility and whether an explanation had been proposed. With prompting, you also corrected the heat-resistance comparison.

Precise meaning and evidence matching

  • Keep the noun, modifier, comparison, and degree unchanged. Knowing the words separately is not enough if an option changes their relationship. This appeared in both the disco and coral review.

Modifier control

  • Find the main clause, lock each modifier to its logical target, and check active or passive meaning. Use the same routine even when the sentence feels familiar.

Research and questionnaire reasoning

  • Separate hypothesis, procedure, measurement, and result. Ask whether prior knowledge or another explanation could account for the finding. Practise linking validity to the specific design issue in the passage.

For your next attempt

  • Before checking an answer, identify the controlling phrase or structure and explain why the closest alternative fails.
  • Use new passages across literature, social science, and natural science to check whether the method transfers.
  • Review the written agreement responses before deciding which grammar points need teaching.
